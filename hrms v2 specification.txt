## Document purpose and scope
This document defines a proposed navigation structure, role-based sidebar, page-by-page features, data fields, tables, forms, actions, approval boundaries, and shared interface conventions for the HRMS. It is intended to guide requirements validation, wireframing, database design, and implementation.
The six core modules are Performance Management, Competency Management, Learning Management, Training Management, Succession Planning, and Social Recognition. Supporting pages cover employee/job setup, administration, AI recommendations, notifications, reports, and auditability.

## 1. Global application layout and design conventions
### 1.1 Standard shell
- Area: Left sidebar; Required content / behavior: Role-specific navigation, expandable module menus, active-page indicator, collapsed mode where useful.
- Area: Top bar; Required content / behavior: Page title or breadcrumbs, global search, notifications, help, profile menu.
- Area: Main content; Required content / behavior: Page header, short description, primary action, summary cards, filters, tables/forms/charts.
- Area: Detail panel/page; Required content / behavior: Record summary, related records, evidence, comments, history, allowed actions.
- Area: Feedback states; Required content / behavior: Loading, empty, validation error, access denied, successful save, failed operation.
- Area: Pagination; Required content / behavior: Record count, page controls, page size, sorting, and filter persistence where practical.
- Area: Responsive behavior; Required content / behavior: Sidebar collapses on narrow screens; tables can scroll or switch to cards; forms remain usable.
### 1.2 Common page structure
Page header: title, breadcrumb, one-sentence purpose, and the main permitted action.
Summary cards: counts relevant to the current role and data scope.
Filter row: keyword search and relevant filters such as department, job title, review period, date, and status.
Main content: data table, card list, chart, form, calendar, or matrix.
Detail view: linked records, evidence, comments, status history, and audit metadata.
Footer controls: pagination, record count, export where authorized, and last-updated information when useful.
### 1.3 UI consistency standards
- Component: Tables; Standard: Search, sort, pagination, filters, clear empty state, row-level actions.
- Component: Forms; Standard: Labels, required indicators, field validation, helpful error messages, save draft where appropriate.
- Component: Status badges; Standard: Use consistent labels and colors across modules; never rely on color alone.
- Component: Ratings; Standard: Show the rating scale and definition; distinguish missing/unassessed from low ratings.
- Component: AI recommendations; Standard: Show the source gap, rationale, suggested action, reviewer, decision, and timestamp.
- Component: Charts; Standard: Show date range, population, labels, and a text/table alternative.
- Component: Files; Standard: Show filename, type, uploader, date, and access restrictions.
- Component: Audit trail; Standard: Record actor, action, record, timestamp, and before/after values where appropriate.
- Component: Security; Standard: Enforce permissions on the server/API as well as hiding unauthorized sidebar links.

## 3. Module 1 — Performance Management
Purpose: provide a standardized performance-management process while allowing the actual evaluation content to vary according to the employee’s department, job position, responsibilities, KPIs, competencies, and applicable safety or qualification requirements. The module incorporates the client’s existing appraisal process as the practical baseline and extends it into a configurable, evidence-based performance and development workflow.
Design principle: standardize the process, not the content. Every employee follows the same evaluation lifecycle, rating methodology, evidence structure, review workflow, and development follow-up, while the criteria presented on the evaluation form are generated from the approved evaluation profile for the employee’s job position.
### 3.1 Client Process Integration
The client’s current non-supervisory performance evaluation form is retained as the real-world baseline rather than copied as a universal checklist. Its established process includes a 1–5 rating scale, evaluator/reviewer roles, employee acknowledgement, performance factors, strengths and weaknesses, development recommendations, advancement/development considerations, and an overall recommendation.
The ten client performance factors are retained as source concepts and are decomposed into common organizational criteria, position-specific performance indicators, and/or job-specific competencies as appropriate.
- Client factor: Quality of Work; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a common concept, but define observable quality indicators according to the position.
- Client factor: Quantity of Work; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Convert to position-specific productivity/output indicators where measurable.
- Client factor: Job Knowledge; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Map to job-specific technical knowledge and competencies.
- Client factor: Ability to Learn; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a development/learning-related behavioral criterion; do not use it as a substitute for job competency.
- Client factor: Dependability; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a common behavioral criterion covering reliability, attendance/punctuality, and responsibility.
- Client factor: Initiative; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a common behavioral competency with role-appropriate examples.
- Client factor: Human Relations/Teamwork; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a common organizational competency; indicators vary by work context.
- Client factor: Cost Consciousness; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Apply where relevant to the position, such as driving, fleet, warehouse, procurement, and operations.
- Client factor: Discipline; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a common compliance/behavior criterion.
- Client factor: Safety Consciousness/Care of Equipment; Treatment in proposed HRMS: Retain as a common principle but make the detailed indicators and criticality position-specific; treat safety-critical requirements separately from the overall average.
### 3.2 Common Evaluation Framework
The evaluation engine uses four layers. The first and fourth layers are standardized for all employees; the middle layers are configured according to the position.
- Layer: A. Employee and process context; Standardized components: Employee ID, department, job title, supervisor, cycle, evaluator, dates, workflow, acknowledgement; Position-specific components: Employee/job values and reporting relationship
- Layer: B. Core organizational performance; Standardized components: Common behavioral/organizational criteria such as dependability, teamwork, communication, initiative, discipline, professionalism, and applicable safety awareness; Position-specific components: Exact observable indicators and applicability may vary by role
- Layer: C. Job performance and competency; Standardized components: Common data structure, 1–5 rating method, evidence fields, comments, approval workflow; Position-specific components: KPIs, responsibilities, technical criteria, competencies, required levels, weights, evidence methods, mandatory/safety-critical requirements
- Layer: D. Development outcome; Standardized components: Strengths, development needs, employee comments, supervisor comments, development goals, acknowledgement, audit history; Position-specific components: Specific development actions, target competencies, learning/training activities, verification criteria, target dates
### 3.3 Standardized vs Position-Specific Evaluation Components
- Component: Evaluation cycle/workflow; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: No
- Component: Rating scale and rating definitions; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: No
- Component: Self-evaluation and supervisor review; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: No
- Component: Evidence/comments/history/audit; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: No
- Component: Strengths and development needs; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: No
- Component: Overall result structure; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: No
- Component: Job responsibilities; Standardized across system: No; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: KPIs/performance indicators; Standardized across system: Framework standardized; content varies; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Technical competencies; Standardized across system: No; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Required competency levels; Standardized across system: No; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Criterion weights; Standardized across system: Configuration rule; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Evidence requirements; Standardized across system: Common field structure; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Mandatory qualifications; Standardized across system: Common tracking structure; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Safety-critical requirements; Standardized across system: Common control structure; Position-specific: Yes
- Component: Development activities; Standardized across system: Common workflow; Position-specific: Specific to identified gaps
- Component: AI recommendation process; Standardized across system: Yes; Position-specific: Inputs are position-specific
### 3.4 Evaluation Item Types
- Item type: Core/organizational criterion; Question answered: How does the employee demonstrate expected workplace behavior?; Examples: Dependability, teamwork, initiative, discipline, communication
- Item type: Job performance/KPI criterion; Question answered: What results or outputs did the employee achieve?; Examples: On-time delivery, picking accuracy, HR transaction accuracy, documentation turnaround
- Item type: Competency criterion; Question answered: What capability does the employee demonstrate while performing the job?; Examples: Defensive driving, inventory management, HR policy application, fleet maintenance knowledge
- Item type: Critical requirement; Question answered: Is a mandatory safety, license, certification, or qualification requirement satisfied?; Examples: Driver license validity, safety qualification, mandatory equipment certification
Performance criteria and competencies must not be treated as identical. A KPI measures an outcome or result; a competency describes the capability demonstrated to achieve work outcomes. A high overall performance score does not automatically eliminate a critical competency or qualification gap.
### 3.5 Position Evaluation Profile and Dynamic Template
The system should use one configurable evaluation engine rather than hard-coded forms for every job. Each approved job position has an Evaluation Profile that determines which common criteria, position-specific criteria, competencies, KPIs, weights, rating descriptors, evidence methods, and critical requirements appear on the employee’s evaluation form.
- Evaluation Profile field: Profile ID/version; Purpose: Controls configuration and historical reproducibility.
- Evaluation Profile field: Job position/department; Purpose: Determines the employee population to which the profile applies.
- Evaluation Profile field: Common criteria; Purpose: Selects applicable organizational criteria.
- Evaluation Profile field: Position-specific criteria/KPIs; Purpose: Defines measurable results and responsibilities for the position.
- Evaluation Profile field: Required competencies; Purpose: Links the position to competency requirements.
- Evaluation Profile field: Required competency level; Purpose: Defines target proficiency for each competency.
- Evaluation Profile field: Criterion weight; Purpose: Defines relative importance when weighted scoring is approved.
- Evaluation Profile field: Rating descriptors; Purpose: Defines observable meaning for 1–5 ratings.
- Evaluation Profile field: Evidence method; Purpose: Defines acceptable evidence such as KPI record, observation, document, certificate, or practical assessment.
- Evaluation Profile field: Mandatory/safety-critical flag; Purpose: Prevents critical requirements from being hidden by an overall average.
- Evaluation Profile field: Effective date/approval; Purpose: Controls when the profile becomes active and preserves history.
Evaluation templates are therefore configurations of the same system component. The system should not create separate software logic for Driver, Warehouse Operator, HR Staff, Fleet Coordinator, or other jobs; it should load the appropriate approved profile when the employee’s position is known.

### 3.7 Rating Scale and Scoring
The client’s 1–5 rating scale is retained as the common organizational scale. The labels should be accompanied by observable definitions so that a rating represents performance against the configured criterion rather than a subjective impression alone.
- Rating: 5; Standard interpretation: Excellent / consistently exceeds expectations; System requirement: Evidence shows sustained performance materially above the defined expectation.
- Rating: 4; Standard interpretation: Above Average / often exceeds expectations; System requirement: Evidence shows performance above the normal requirement with consistency.
- Rating: 3; Standard interpretation: Average / meets expectations; System requirement: Performance meets the defined requirement at the expected level.
- Rating: 2; Standard interpretation: Below Average / improvement required; System requirement: Performance falls below the expected level and requires follow-up.
- Rating: 1; Standard interpretation: Poor / unacceptable; System requirement: Performance is substantially below the requirement or repeatedly fails the defined standard.
If weighted scoring is formally configured, the normalized performance score is calculated as: Final Score = Σ(Rating × Weight) / Σ(Weight). Weights must sum to 100% for a fully configured template. The system must retain the individual criterion ratings and evidence; the overall score is a summary, not a replacement for criterion-level review.
Do not compare raw criterion scores across unrelated positions. Cross-position reporting should compare only equivalent or clearly mapped criteria/scales, while position-level reports may use the complete position-specific profile.
### 3.8 Critical Safety and Qualification Controls
Safety-critical competencies and mandatory qualifications are controlled separately from the overall performance average. A strong overall score must not conceal failure of a mandatory requirement. The system should display the overall performance result together with a separate critical-requirement status.
- Example: Driver safety/license; Performance score behavior: Safety indicators contribute to the configured score; Critical requirement behavior: Expired/missing mandatory license or critical safety qualification is separately flagged and may restrict affected duties according to company rules/law.
- Example: Warehouse equipment certification; Performance score behavior: Safe handling may be scored as a criterion; Critical requirement behavior: Required certification is tracked separately and must be current where applicable.
- Example: General office/HR role; Performance score behavior: Safety awareness may be an organizational criterion if applicable; Critical requirement behavior: Technical safety qualifications are not forced onto the role unless required.
### 3.9 Evaluation Form Page Layout
The Evaluation Form page should be dynamic but visually consistent. The header identifies the employee, department, job position, supervisor, evaluator, cycle, due date, profile version, and status. The body is divided into common criteria, position-specific performance/KPIs, competencies, critical requirements, evidence, and development.
- Section: A. Employee & job context; Fields / behavior: Employee, ID, department, job title, supervisor, cycle, evaluator, profile/version, dates; Required controls: Read-only job context for ordinary evaluators; record-level access control
- Section: B. Common criteria; Fields / behavior: Criterion, definition, 1–5 rating, comments, evidence; Required controls: Show rating definitions; support self and supervisor ratings separately
- Section: C. Position-specific performance; Fields / behavior: KPI/criterion, target/definition, rating, evidence, comments, weight where configured; Required controls: Show only criteria active for the employee’s position
- Section: D. Competencies; Fields / behavior: Competency, required level, current/assessed level, evidence, assessor, method; Required controls: Link to Competency Management; distinguish unassessed from low level
- Section: E. Critical requirements; Fields / behavior: Qualification/certificate, status, expiry, evidence, safety-critical flag; Required controls: Track separately from average; flag action when required
- Section: F. Development outcome; Fields / behavior: Strengths, development needs, employee goals, supervisor recommendations, next steps; Required controls: Create link to development plan and AI recommendation workflow
- Section: G. Review and acknowledgement; Fields / behavior: Self vs supervisor comparison, comments, decision, acknowledgement, approval history; Required controls: Record timestamps, reviewers, revisions, reasons for reopening

### 3.11 Performance Evaluation to Competency Management
Performance evaluation and competency management are related but distinct. Performance evaluation asks how well the employee performed during the review period; competency management asks whether the employee possesses the capability required by the position. A high performance score does not automatically mean that every required competency is at the target level.
- Example: Driver with strong delivery results but weak defensive driving; Performance result: Overall may be high; Competency result: Required Level 4; Current Level 2; Gap 2; System action: Flag safety competency gap; require appropriate human review and development action.
- Example: Warehouse employee completes tasks quickly but has poor handling competency; Performance result: Productivity may be high; Competency result: Required Level 3; Current Level 1; System action: Do not close gap based on productivity; assess and address the competency.
- Example: HR staff demonstrates strong policy knowledge but misses HR deadlines; Performance result: Competency may be adequate; Competency result: Required Level 4; Current Level 4; System action: Address performance/process issue through feedback and development rather than inventing a competency gap.
For comparable numeric competency levels, the existing Competency Management rule applies: Gap = max(0, Required Level − Current Level). Missing current ratings remain unassessed, and mandatory qualification issues are tracked separately.
### 3.12 Performance → Competency → Gemini → Development Workflow
The integrated performance-development cycle is:
1. Maintain the approved job description, responsibilities, KPIs, required competencies, target levels, evidence methods, and mandatory qualifications for the position.
2. HR opens an evaluation cycle and assigns self-evaluation and supervisor review according to policy.
3. The system loads the employee’s approved position Evaluation Profile and displays common plus position-specific criteria.
4. Employee completes self-evaluation where enabled; supervisor completes the authorized evaluation using evidence.
5. The system calculates criterion and overall performance results where weights are configured.
6. Supervisor or qualified assessor validates relevant competencies against the position requirements.
7. The system calculates competency gaps and separately flags missing or mandatory qualifications.
8. Validated, authorized performance/development evidence and competency gaps become inputs to Gemini 2.5 Flash.
9. Gemini proposes gap-linked learning/training recommendations with rationale, target competency, activity, and verification method.
10. Supervisor and/or Operations Manager (Training & Development Officer) reviews, edits, accepts, or rejects the recommendation.
11. Accepted recommendations become development plans and learning/training assignments.
12. Learning Management records learning and knowledge checks; Training Management records practical training, attendance, and results.
13. The defined competency assessment method is repeated after development.
14. The system updates the gap based on evidence. Training completion alone does not close a competency gap.
15. Unresolved gaps receive further development actions, reassessment, or escalation according to policy.
### 3.13 Gemini Recommendation Input and Output Controls
- Input to Gemini: Employee job/position requirements; Required control: Use approved role requirements only; minimize personal data; Expected structured output: Relevant competency/performance gap
- Input to Gemini: Validated competency ratings and evidence; Required control: Do not send unvalidated or unauthorized ratings; Expected structured output: Rationale linked to evidence
- Input to Gemini: Relevant development needs from evaluation; Required control: Use current review-period needs where relevant; Expected structured output: Target competency and target level
- Input to Gemini: Completed learning/training history; Required control: Use to avoid redundant recommendations; Expected structured output: Proposed learning/training activity
- Input to Gemini: Available mapped courses/resources; Required control: Validate that resources exist; Expected structured output: Mapped course/resource when available
- Input to Gemini: Verification method; Required control: Require a way to determine whether the gap improved; Expected structured output: Reassessment/practical verification method
Gemini is a recommendation service, not a human role. It must not invent performance data, create unsupported competency ratings, silently assign courses, or make disciplinary, promotion, or succession decisions. Human reviewers remain responsible for consequential HR decisions.
### 3.14 Development Planning Outputs
- Development plan field: Linked evaluation/gap; Purpose: Traceability from performance evidence to development need.
- Development plan field: Current vs target competency; Purpose: Defines the capability gap being addressed.
- Development plan field: Development objective; Purpose: States what improvement is expected.
- Development plan field: Activities; Purpose: Courses, coaching, mentoring, supervised practice, job rotation, practical training, or other approved activities.
- Development plan field: Owner/trainer; Purpose: Identifies responsibility for the action.
- Development plan field: Target date; Purpose: Defines expected completion/review timing.
- Development plan field: Success/verification criteria; Purpose: Defines how improvement will be demonstrated.
- Development plan field: Reassessment date/result; Purpose: Determines whether the gap is resolved.
- Development plan field: Status/history; Purpose: Preserves progress and auditability.
Training completion is recorded as a learning/training outcome, while competency attainment is determined by the defined assessment method. A course may be completed while the related competency gap remains open.
### 3.15 Reporting and Cross-Position Comparability
The system should support organization-wide reporting without pretending that unrelated job criteria are directly comparable. Standardized reports should focus on common process measures and clearly mapped metrics. Position-level reports should preserve the context of the position-specific criteria.
- Reporting level: Organization; Recommended metrics: Evaluation completion; overall rating distribution; common competency gaps; development-plan completion; training completion; reassessment outcomes.
- Reporting level: Department; Recommended metrics: Evaluation completion; performance trends for comparable criteria; competency gaps by category; training needs; unresolved critical gaps.
- Reporting level: Job position; Recommended metrics: Position-specific KPI/criterion results; required/current competency levels; gap patterns; development outcomes.
- Reporting level: Employee; Recommended metrics: Criterion ratings, evidence, feedback, competency history, open gaps, development plan, training and reassessment history.
Historical trend charts must compare equivalent or clearly mapped criteria and scales. A Driver “On-Time Delivery” result should not be treated as directly equivalent to an HR “Recruitment Turnaround” result merely because both use a 1–5 rating.

- Record: Development Plan; Key relationships / fields: Employee; linked gap; objective; activities; owner; dates; success criteria; status; approval; reassessment result.
### 3.17 Performance Management Rules and Exceptions
- Scenario: Employee and supervisor ratings differ; Required system behavior: Retain both ratings and comments; route for discussion/review according to policy; do not silently overwrite one rating.
- Scenario: Evaluation profile changes after a cycle opens; Required system behavior: Do not silently change existing evaluations; preserve the profile version and audit the change.
- Scenario: No KPI is available for a criterion; Required system behavior: Use an approved observable criterion/evidence method rather than inventing a numeric value.
- Scenario: Missing evidence; Required system behavior: Allow return/request-evidence workflow; do not treat missing evidence as a low rating automatically.
- Scenario: Safety-critical failure; Required system behavior: Flag separately from overall score and apply the company’s defined action/restriction rules.
- Scenario: Training completed but reassessment fails; Required system behavior: Record completion but keep the competency gap unresolved; create further development/reassessment action.
- Scenario: No suitable mapped learning resource; Required system behavior: Training Officer selects another approved intervention or requests content development; do not invent a course record.
- Scenario: AI unavailable; Required system behavior: Allow authorized manual development planning or safe retry while preserving source data and status.
- Scenario: Unauthorized access; Required system behavior: Deny access and record a security-relevant event according to policy.
### 3.18 Performance Management Validation Checklist
☐ Client’s 1–5 rating scale is retained and rating definitions are displayed.

☐ One dynamic evaluation framework is used instead of separate hard-coded forms per job.
☐ Each approved job position has an Evaluation Profile with effective version and approval status.
☐ Common organizational criteria are separated from position-specific KPIs and competencies.
☐ Job-specific competencies are linked to required proficiency levels.
☐ Position-specific KPIs/criteria have definitions, evidence methods, and approved weights where applicable.
☐ Safety-critical and mandatory qualification requirements are tracked separately from the overall score.
☐ Self-evaluation and supervisor evaluation can be retained separately.
☐ Criterion-level evidence, comments, history, and audit trail are retained.
☐ Performance results feed validated competency assessment and gap analysis without conflating the two.

☐ Human reviewers accept, edit, or reject AI recommendations before assignment.
☐ Accepted recommendations can become development plans and learning/training assignments.
☐ Training completion is not treated as proof of competency attainment.
☐ Competency reassessment determines whether a gap is resolved.
☐ Reports compare only equivalent or clearly mapped criteria/scales across positions.
☐ Promotion, succession, disciplinary, and other consequential decisions are not made solely from AI output or an overall score.

## 5. Gemini 2.5 Flash integration and AI recommendation review
Gemini is an application service, not a human user role. The application should supply validated, authorized information and treat the model output as a recommendation requiring review.
Collect the employee's relevant job requirements, validated competency ratings, supporting evidence, relevant performance development needs, completed learning, and available mapped courses.
Validate required fields, rating definitions, data freshness, and course availability before sending the request.
Send only the information needed for the recommendation; minimize personal data.
Request a structured output that identifies the competency gap, recommendation, rationale, target competency, proposed learning activity, and verification method.
Validate output structure and confirm that suggested courses or resources exist. Flag unsupported or unmapped recommendations.
Display the result to the authorized Supervisor or Training Officer for review.
Record accept/edit/reject decision, reviewer, reason where required, model/version metadata if available, and timestamp.
Convert accepted recommendations into a development plan and link it to the originating gap.
After learning/training, reassess the competency and update the gap status based on evidence.

### 5.3 AI safeguards
AI does not invent performance data or assign unsubstantiated competency ratings.
AI suggestions must map to an identified gap and, where possible, to real learning resources.
Human reviewers decide whether recommendations are appropriate and feasible.
Keep an audit trail of recommendation, decision, edits, and later outcomes.
Do not use AI output alone to make disciplinary, promotion, or succession decisions.
Apply access control, data minimization, retention rules, and company privacy requirements.

## 13. Permissions, audit, privacy, and exception handling
### 13.1 Approval boundaries
- Action: Create accounts and assign permissions; Performer: Super Admin / Admin (within assigned permissions); Reviewer / approval boundary: Super Admin governs roles and permissions; Admin handles routine account administration.
- Action: Maintain employee/job records; Performer: HR Administrator; Reviewer / approval boundary: Department Head or designated approver where policy requires.
- Action: Complete self-evaluation; Performer: Employee; Reviewer / approval boundary: Assigned supervisor reviews.
- Action: Rate employee performance; Performer: Supervisor; Reviewer / approval boundary: HR Administrator reviews according to policy.
- Action: Validate competency evidence; Performer: Supervisor / qualified assessor; Reviewer / approval boundary: Designated reviewer if required.
- Action: Generate AI suggestions; Performer: Application + Gemini service; Reviewer / approval boundary: Supervisor / Operations Manager reviews recommendations.
- Action: Publish learning content; Performer: Operations Manager (Training & Development Officer); Reviewer / approval boundary: HR Administrator or designated content approver, if configured.
- Action: Record training completion; Performer: Employee / trainer / Training Officer; Reviewer / approval boundary: Supervisor verifies practical competency where applicable.
- Action: Nominate recognition; Performer: Employee / supervisor / permitted colleague; Reviewer / approval boundary: Designated recognition approver.
- Action: Review succession candidates; Performer: HR Administrator / Supervisor; Reviewer / approval boundary: Department Head reviews or approves where required.
### 13.2 Exception handling
- Scenario: Evaluation incomplete; System behavior: Flag missing fields or overdue task; notify responsible person; keep status incomplete.
- Scenario: Employee and supervisor ratings differ; System behavior: Retain both ratings and comments; route discussion/review according to policy.
- Scenario: AI recommendation unsupported; System behavior: Flag for human review; do not auto-assign; allow rejection or correction of source data.
- Scenario: No suitable learning content; System behavior: Training Officer selects coaching, practical training, or requests content creation; gap stays open.
- Scenario: Training completed but reassessment failed; System behavior: Record course completion but leave competency gap unresolved; plan additional practice or reassessment.
- Scenario: Mandatory qualification expired/missing; System behavior: Flag for action; restrict affected duties when company rules or law require it.
- Scenario: AI service unavailable; System behavior: Allow authorized manual recommendation/plan workflow or retry safely; preserve input and error status.
- Scenario: Unauthorized record access; System behavior: Deny access and record security-relevant event according to policy.
### 13.3 Privacy and security
Apply least-privilege access and record-level scope by role.
Limit AI inputs to information necessary for the task; follow company privacy and retention policies.
Protect performance reviews, personal records, and succession information from public feeds and broad exports.
Keep audit history for important record changes, approvals, AI suggestions, and exports.
Make sure the UI and server/API enforce the same authorization rules.
Do not treat AI recommendations as authoritative facts or as the sole basis for consequential employment decisions.
Use human verification for safety-critical competency and qualification requirements.

## Final design recommendation
The application should use role-based navigation and a shared data model across all six core modules. Its central cycle is: performance evidence → competency assessment → gap analysis → Gemini-assisted personalized recommendations → approved learning/training → reassessment. Social Recognition and Succession Planning are related but distinct outcomes and should not be mandatory steps for every employee. The seven retained roles absorb the former HR Manager, Learning Content Manager, Senior Management, and HR Analyst / Report Viewer responsibilities; reporting and approvals remain permission-scoped.
Before implementation, validate role names, approval rules, scoring scales, evaluation criteria, mandatory qualifications, and reporting requirements with the partner freight and logistics company. Treat all sample values and workflow statuses in this document as proposed configuration until approved.
